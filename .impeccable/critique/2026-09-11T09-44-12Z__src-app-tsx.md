## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment:** The public homepage does not read as AI-generated. It has a clear point of view: official brand artwork, natural workplace photography, a restrained three-colour system, and alternating service chapters. The main risk is implementation drift rather than aesthetic genericness: older CSS still contains pill, card and accent-border treatments that can compete with the approved direction if they become active again.

**Deterministic scan:** One warning was found: `border-accent-on-rounded` at `src/App.css:4271` on `.who-founders div`. That selector is not used by the current homepage JSX, so it is a dead-legacy false positive for the public surface. It should still be removed or isolated with the rest of the unused styles.

## Overall Impression

The site feels credible, established and recognisably OccUhealth. The largest opportunity is to make the type system feel authored as one system: the live page currently jumps from a roughly 99px hero heading to a roughly 80px “One partner” heading, then to roughly 51px service and programme headings, while body copy ranges from 16px to 19.5px. That hierarchy is visually louder and less deliberate than the design guide describes.

## What's Working

- The correct brand fonts are loaded and actively rendered: Nexa for headings and Neue Haas Unica W1G for body copy.
- The blue/off-white/green palette, official symbols and workplace photography make the service offer feel specific rather than like a generic healthcare template.
- Image handling is thoughtful: meaningful alt text, explicit dimensions, a high-priority hero image and lazy loading for below-the-fold imagery.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Older CSS overrides the approved typography

**Why it matters:** This is the clearest source of the inconsistent sizing the user is noticing. `#who-we-are h2` in `src/App.css:3836-3839` has higher specificity than the intended `.who-final__copy h2` rule in `src/brand-alignment.css:413-417`, so the live “One partner” heading renders at about 79.9px instead of the intended 3rem cap. `src/components/Hero.css:140-145` is imported after the brand alignment file and sets the hero to a 5.8rem maximum; it renders at about 98.6px on the inspected desktop viewport, above the design guide’s 4rem cap.

**Fix:** Consolidate the live type scale into one token block, remove the stale high-specificity rules, and keep the hero/section caps in one authoritative stylesheet. Recheck at desktop, tablet and 320–390px widths.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable typeset`, then `$impeccable polish`

### [P1] Section semantics and accent contrast need a pass

**Why it matters:** The Services section in `src/components/Services.tsx:37-78` has no section label or visible h2, so the first service is announced as an h3 without a parent heading. The Contact section in `src/components/ContactCTA.tsx:37-115` similarly begins with an unlabeled section and uses h3 for both the team introduction and each person. That weakens scanability and screen-reader structure. Separately, lime `#AAC334` on off-white `#F6F8F9` measures about 1.87:1, below WCAG AA even for large text, so the lime accent headings in the Who We Are and Value sections are not accessible as text.

**Fix:** Add visible h2/`aria-labelledby` structure for Services and Contact, and use cobalt for text on off-white with lime reserved for a background, rule, icon or other non-text accent. Cobalt on off-white is a strong 6.61:1.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`, then `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Copy style is not fully editorially consistent

**Why it matters:** Navigation says “About us” while the footer says “Who We Are”; calls to action use title case (“Speak to Our Team Today”) while other headings use sentence case. Founder roles use “Co-Founder · Director” in the About section but “Co-Founder | Director” in the contact panel. “Compliance focused” should be hyphenated as “Compliance-focused”; the unused `proofSignals` data also contains “Compliant Focused”. Small inconsistencies like these make an otherwise careful B2B brand feel assembled rather than edited.

**Fix:** Choose sentence case for navigation, buttons and descriptive headings; standardise the middle dot role separator; do one copy pass for hyphenation, apostrophes and acronym expansion.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] The CSS system is carrying too many competing generations

**Why it matters:** One homepage is backed by approximately 4,579 lines in `src/App.css`, 1,619 in `src/brand-alignment.css` and 1,003 in `src/redesign.css`, with a compiled CSS asset of about 147KB. The active page is mostly tokenised, but `src/components/WhoWeAreFinal.css` still hard-codes the brand colours and unused legacy selectors remain in the cascade. This is what allowed the typography override above to survive and makes future consistency regressions likely.

**Fix:** Separate public-page styles from concept-gallery styles, delete unreachable selectors, replace remaining active literals with brand tokens, and leave one final override layer rather than layering multiple redesigns.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`, then `$impeccable polish`

### [P2] Responsive verification is incomplete

**Why it matters:** The source has sensible breakpoints and mostly meets 44px interaction targets, but a narrow-viewport render was not available in the browser session. The highest-risk areas to verify are the fluid hero heading, founder name captions, the two-column capability list and the contact panel transitions at 620px and 390px.

**Fix:** Test 320px, 375px, 768px and desktop widths plus 200% text zoom. Confirm there is no heading wrap collision, clipped contact address, or unreadably small founder role text.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`, then `$impeccable audit`

## Persona Red Flags

**HR leader / business owner:** The overall proposition is clear, but the first service chapter begins without an “Our services” heading. They have to infer the section from the nav label and the first service title, which makes the page slower to scan.

**EHS decision-maker:** Terms such as DSE/VDU, SCORE2, PHQ-4, MSK and ESG appear without short explanations. The expertise is credible, but the page occasionally makes the buyer translate clinical shorthand instead of understanding the business outcome.

**First-time mobile visitor:** The navigation and CTA strategy are good, but the page needs a narrow-width/zoom pass. At small sizes, founder role captions are deliberately reduced to roughly 0.68–0.70rem and the contact section contains several dense address and phone blocks.

## Minor Observations

- The founders appear Cathy-first in the About section but Sharon-first in the contact list; matching the order would reinforce recognition.
- The Footer h2 headings compute at the same 17px size as body copy, so the footer column hierarchy is weaker than the main page hierarchy.
- The contact section duplicates the email action in “Start the conversation” and the utility grid. That is acceptable for conversion, but the two labels should be clearly differentiated in tone and purpose.
- The public page passes basic image-content checks: all inspected content images have useful alt text and intrinsic dimensions.

## Questions to Consider

- Should the hero and “One partner” headings stay intentionally oversized, or should they follow the design guide’s tighter 4rem/3rem caps?
- Is the priority to fix typography/accessibility first, or to do the copy/editorial consistency pass first?
- Should the cleanup cover the whole CSS stack, or focus on the public homepage files only?
